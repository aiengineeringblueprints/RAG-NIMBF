# Incident Report INC-2026-318

Owner: Kestrel Instruments. Classification: internal.

## Overview

Incident INC-2026-318 interrupted measurements at Nessby Kraft GmbH and was handled by customer support together with engineering.

This report documents incident INC-2026-318 at Nessby Kraft GmbH. It follows the blameless postmortem format and focuses on causes and improvements, not on individuals.

Incident INC-2026-318 occurred on 12 September 2025 at Nessby Kraft GmbH. The affected product was the Kestrel KL9 and the incident was classified as severity 3.

## Customer impact

Data gaps caused by incident INC-2026-318 were documented and shared with the customer.

The customer had to fall back on manual readings while incident INC-2026-318 was open.

Measurements at Nessby Kraft GmbH were unavailable or unreliable during the incident. No safety functions were affected.

The final total downtime was 422 minutes.

## Detection

Incident INC-2026-318 was detected when Nessby Kraft GmbH noticed missing values in the control system and opened a support case.

A technician of Nessby Kraft GmbH reported unusual readings during a routine inspection, which started incident INC-2026-318.

## Root cause

Root cause analysis identified a firmware memory leak.

## Customer communication

A written summary of incident INC-2026-318 was sent to Nessby Kraft GmbH together with this report.

Nessby Kraft GmbH received status updates from customer support throughout incident INC-2026-318.

After incident INC-2026-318 the account manager held a review meeting with Nessby Kraft GmbH to explain causes and actions.

## Other factors

- The affected installation was not on the latest firmware
- Monitoring did not alert on the first symptoms
- Spare devices were not available on site
- The fault was first mistaken for a network problem

## Actions

| Action | Owner | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Ship the fix with firmware 3.7.9 | Engineering | done |
| Review spare part stock for key customers | Logistics | done |
| Share lessons learned in the engineering forum | Engineering | open |

The fix shipped with firmware 3.7.9.

## What we learned

- Remote diagnostics access was essential
- Checklists need regular review after incidents
- Early escalation shortened the investigation

## Follow-up

The knowledge base was updated with the diagnosis steps used during incident INC-2026-318.

Similar installations at other customers were checked for the conditions that led to incident INC-2026-318.

The effectiveness of the corrective actions for incident INC-2026-318 will be checked in the next quality review.
